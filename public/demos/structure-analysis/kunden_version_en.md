# Customer Version

Here is your structure analysis – your documented current state at this point in time.

### The Central Pattern

The central theme of your structure is not a simple forward or backward lean, but rather a segmented organization – a kind of "Z-shape". Your body interrupts the flow of force at several points, instead of allowing the major body segments to rest relaxed on top of each other. This creates particularly high stress at one point: the **thoracolumbar junction**, where the thoracic and lumbar spine meet. This area becomes a kind of hinge.

### The Connections

The foundation of this pattern lies in the position of your pelvis. It is in a **posterior tilt** – tilted backward. The consequence is that your lower lumbar spine loses its natural, shock-absorbing curve and becomes rather flat. The visual impression of a hollow back is deceptive. The visible curve does not originate deep in the lower back, but rather as a sharp "kink" at the aforementioned transition to the thoracic spine. This area must compensate for the stiff base below.

Above this, your ribcage reacts by tilting significantly backward as a counterweight. This is not an active, upright posture, but rather a passive "hanging" into the structures. Your sternum sinks inward – referred to as a **collapsed sternum**. This position forms a descending base for the neck and head.

At the very top, your head must compensate for this chain to keep your gaze level. It shifts significantly forward, resulting in an **anterior head posture**. The high tension in the neck and pressure at the skull base are a direct consequence of this.

### What This Means for the Body

Imagine your spine losing its ability to act like a spring due to this segmented posture. The load that would normally be distributed along its entire length now concentrates on this one hinge point at the thoracolumbar junction.

This is an intelligent strategy by your body to find balance in everyday life. However, the price is high and uneven mechanical stress. This transitional area carries many times the load it was designed for. Additionally, the backward-tilted ribcage with the sunken sternum restricts the space for deep, free breathing.

### Assessment and Outlook

This is your current state. These patterns have developed over years and are changeable. The program gives you the tools to work on these issues (continuously) and help your body redistribute the load more evenly.

Do another analysis in a few weeks and compare the results – this way you can see what has changed and draw conclusions about the ongoing focus for you and your structural transformation.
