# Leads Version

Thank you for your trust and the opportunity to look at your structure. It's not a given to show yourself so openly.

Here's what I see when I move through your body from bottom to top:

Your knees push slightly forward. However, your body doesn't respond by tipping forward as a whole. Instead, your thighs actively pull backward. This is the first interruption in the force flow, like a small kink in the line.

Above that, your pelvis tilts backward. As a result, your lumbar spine loses its natural, gentle inward curve and becomes rather flat. If at first glance there's an impression of a hollow back, that's a visual illusion. The curve you see doesn't originate deep in the lower back, but is instead a sharp "kink" much higher up – at the so-called thoracolumbar junction, where the thoracic spine meets the lumbar spine. This junction must compensate for the missing curve below.

Above this kink, your ribcage leans significantly backward. This isn't an upright, proud posture, but rather a passive leaning into the ligaments and fascia. At the same time, your sternum sinks inward somewhat, which restricts the space for deep, free breathing in the front of the chest.

At the very top, your head must compensate for all of this so your gaze goes straight ahead. To do this, it shifts forward. Something special happens here: Your neck hyperextends right at the skull base, while your lower jaw pulls slightly backward. This creates high tension directly under your skull and at the transition from the neck to the back.

**The Overall Picture:**
Your body isn't simply tilted forward or backward. It has formed a kind of Z-shape: knees forward – hips back – ribcage back – head forward again. Instead of the major body parts stacking like building blocks on top of each other, they hold each other in balance through constant muscular tension. All the load collects at the kink points, the "hinges".

**What This Means:**
Your body hasn't done anything wrong here. It has developed a truly clever strategy to keep you stable and functional in everyday life despite these tensions. The price is that these hinge points – one in the mid-back and one in the neck – carry an enormous load, much more than intended. The ability of your spine to act like a spring is partially lost in the process.

The most important thing is: These patterns are not set in stone. They are a learned response from your nervous system, and they are changeable. Your body has an incredible ability to reorganize itself when given the right conditions.
