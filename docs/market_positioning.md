# Market Positioning — Johannes Schulz

## Core Identity

**Title:** Fullstack Engineer *(no "Senior" prefix — let portfolio signal seniority)*
**Engagement type:** Contractor only, invoiced via US LLC
**Availability:** ~25h/week, fractional (not "part-time")
**Location:** Remote, async-first — available for European and US East Coast time zones
**Languages:** German (C2), English (C2)
**Rate:** ~80€/hr (room to push toward 90–100€ as positioning sharpens)

---

## The One-Sentence Pitch

> "I build and ship fullstack products — from frontend to backend to infrastructure, including AI integration — for teams that need to move fast."

This works for both startup founders and engineering managers at established companies. It makes no seniority claim, treats AI as a natural part of the stack, and leads with outcome.

---

## Target Audience

**Primary sweet spot:** Growth-stage companies (20–100 people). Enough process to work comfortably with contractors, not so rigid that credential verification becomes a gate.

**Also attractive to:**
- Early-stage startups where there is genuine mission alignment (selective, high-effort applications — not bulk pipeline)
- Established companies with defined contractor workflows and clear scopes

**Structural constraints to be aware of:**
- Early-stage startups sometimes want embedded, near-full-time commitment — the 25h/week contractor structure can be a harder sell when they're making foundational decisions
- Large enterprises may have stricter seniority banding and credential verification

**The positioning should not reference either startups or established companies explicitly** — outcome language attracts both without narrowing the audience.

## What I Deliver (Service Framing)

Lead with outcomes, not technologies:

1. **Fullstack Engineering** — features, refactors, embedded delivery (React/Next.js, Python/FastAPI, Node.js, PostgreSQL)
2. **AI Integration** — LLM-powered features, API integration, agentic workflows, RAG pipelines where justified. Backed by an M.Sc. in Machine Learning (Tübingen) and hands-on experience with OpenCLAW infrastructure, structural analysis, and custom agentic systems including CAPS (Claude Automated Programming System)
3. **Stack Setup & Platform** — setting up entire stacks for early-stage products: frontend, API, database, CI/CD, deployment, monitoring, cloud infra (AWS/Docker)
4. **Tech Lead / Architect (Fractional)** — system design, architectural decisions, code reviews, async technical leadership

## How to Frame AI Tooling & Expertise

There are two distinct things to communicate, and they need different treatment:

**1. AI integration as a service** (deliver this as part of fullstack work)
Frame it as part of the stack, not a specialty: *"Modern fullstack engineering includes LLM integration — I've done this with OpenCLAW, RAG pipelines, and custom agentic infrastructure."* The M.Sc. in ML from Tübingen is the credibility signal that separates this from a typical fullstack contractor adding a ChatGPT API call.

**2. AI-augmented workflow** (how you work, not what you sell)
Never in a headline or skills list. Once, quietly, on website About or bio:
> "I use agentic coding tools as part of my daily workflow — they make me faster. Architecture, decisions, and code review remain mine."

The CAPS system (Claude Automated Programming System) is worth a visible mention on your website — not as a product pitch, but as a signal. Building infrastructure *on top of* Claude Code demonstrates a level of agentic workflow understanding that most engineers don't have.

**In practice:** let delivery speed and project outcomes be the proof. The M.Sc. and CAPS do the credibility work in the background.

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- "Fullstack Developer" alone — reads junior
- "AI Engineer" or "AI Systems Engineer" as primary title — wrong work typecast
- "Part-time" — undersells the engagement model
- "Founding Engineer" without a client offering it — credibility risk
- Listing every tool in your stack — pick 4–6 you actually use daily
- Mentioning Claude Code, Cursor, or similar tools in headlines or skills lists
- Leading any application with AI skills

## Regional Notes

### DACH (Germany, Austria, Switzerland)
- Strongest market for you — Germany is the most supply-constrained developer market in Western Europe, actively hiring cross-border
- Target rate: **80–100€/hr**
- Key platforms: **freelancermap.de**, **freelance.de**, LinkedIn, XING
- Write LinkedIn in English; add a German secondary version with keywords: `Softwareentwickler`, `Freiberufler`, `B2B-Vertrag`, `Remote`
- Include a clean headshot (professional norm in DACH)
- US LLC invoicing works well — clearly positions you as a B2B vendor, reduces Scheinselbstständigkeit risk
- State timezone as a strength: *"Remote, async-first — available for European and US East Coast time zones"*

### UK
- **Quote day rates, not hourly** — UK contractor market strongly prefers this convention
- Equivalent day rate: **~€640–750/day** at current positioning
- Key platform: LinkedIn; also Toptal, Contra for senior contractors
- IR35 legislation is the UK equivalent of Scheinselbstständigkeit — invoicing via LLC helps here too

### US & International
- US clients move faster, are more informal, and are very comfortable with async remote contractors
- Your LLC structure is a natural fit for US Corp-to-Corp (C2C) engagements — no friction
- Push rate higher for US clients: **$90–110/hr** is defensible given the market
- Key platforms: LinkedIn, Toptal, Contra, arc.dev
- English-only positioning is fine — no localisation needed
